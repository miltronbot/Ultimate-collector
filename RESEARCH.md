# Homeschool App Market Research & Competitive Analysis

Research compiled for Savannah's Homeschool Dashboard — a Texas-first homeschool platform.

## The Competition and Why It Falls Short for Texas Homeschool Families

| App | What it is | Why it fails Texas homeschoolers |
|-----|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Khan Academy Kids | Free content library | Generic; no curriculum planning, no compliance tracking, no TEKS alignment |
| IXL | Skill practice ($$) | Expensive per-subject pricing; no parent oversight reports; no hour logging |
| Seesaw | Classroom portfolio tool | Built for classrooms, awkward for home; student data privacy concerns |
| ClassDojo | Behavior tracking | Classroom behavior model is irrelevant at home |
| Google Classroom | Assignment LMS | Designed for teachers managing 30 kids, not parents teaching one |
| Schoology / PowerSchool | District LMS / SIS | Administrative overkill; priced and built for districts |
| Homeschool Planet, HST+ | Homeschool planners | Dated UX; planning-only — no student-facing learning activities |

## Top Pain Points (from homeschool parent communities)

1. **Cost fragmentation** — families stack $50–$200/month of subscriptions across 4–6 apps
2. **Data silos** — practice in one app, planning in another, records in a spreadsheet; nothing connects
3. **No state-specific compliance help** — Texas families must self-document instruction; no app tracks it
4. **No TEKS alignment** — content isn't mapped to the standards Texas uses
5. **Parent workload** — planning, logging, and reporting are all manual
6. **Data loss & lock-in** — cloud apps lose data, change pricing, or shut down; no export
7. **Privacy** — most apps harvest student data
8. **Static progress views** — practice doesn't update mastery; parents can't see what's actually sticking
9. **Meaningless gamification** — points and confetti that don't map to real skills

## Texas Legal Context (Leeper framework)

- Texas homeschools operate as private schools; requirements: bona fide instruction, visual curriculum, covering reading, spelling, grammar, math, and good citizenship
- No mandated hour count, but 25.5 hr/week (matching public school instructional time) is the defensible documentation standard many families target
- No state reporting, but records protect families during district inquiries and support re-enrollment/transcripts
- TEKS is the Texas standards framework — aligning to it keeps a student on grade level relative to peers
